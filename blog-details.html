What to wear: Wardrobe guidelines for matching portraits

Written by Olivia Stone • August 5, 2026 • 8 Min Read

Coordinating outfits for a group or family session is often the most stressful part of the planning process. However, focusing on texture coordination and curated tones over perfect uniform matches makes your family portraits feel organic, cinematic, and timeless.

Often, when people think about family photoshoots, they immediately imagine matching white t-shirts and denim jeans. While popular in the early 2000s, this approach tends to strip images of their natural character, leaving cards looking like corporate catalogue placeholders rather than real, warm memories. Here are our four core rules for modern editorial styling.

1. Build Around a Solid Neutral Base

Instead of choosing a single color, pick a palette of 3-4 coordinating tones. Warm ivory, cream, soft camel, olive green, toasted clay, and terracotta work beautifully under natural sunlight. These earth pigments bounce soft light back into faces, helping to establish our signature warm cinematic look.

2. Incorporate Texture and Layers

If everyone wears solid flat cotton shirts, the image can lack depth. Try combining linen shirts, soft knitted sweaters, ribbed fabrics, silk details, or structured coats. Layering vests or light jackets allows kids to modify their look during the session easily without requiring a complete wardrobe change.

"The goal is not to dress like twins, but to dress like you belong in the same painting together."

3. Say No to Neon and Harsh Branding

Bright primary pigments like neon pink, high-visibility orange, or saturated lime greens draw the camera's focus entirely away from your expressions and can reflect colored shadows back onto your skin. Similarly, avoid shirts with prominent sports logos, commercial text prints, or tiny parallel line patterns that create moiré waves in camera sensors.
